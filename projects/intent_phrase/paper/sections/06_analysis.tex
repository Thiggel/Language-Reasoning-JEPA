\section{What geometry is learned?}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{The current evidence is local and incomplete}

GAR makes a local action-ordering claim. In a one-seed aligned-state audit, the deployed transition-value energy attains $.826$ pairwise ranking accuracy and $.786$ top-1 optimal-action recall. Raw predicted successor distance attains $.603$ and $.549$. The learned energy is therefore more decision useful than uncalibrated latent distance in this checkpoint.

This result does not yet prove that GAR reshapes the representation. The transition-value head may learn a supervised readout while the underlying geometry changes little. The one-seed gradient-routing controls are consistent with both effects. Full predictive GAR reaches $.435$ strict success, while detaching GAR gradients from the predictive body reaches $.375$. Raw-distance selection reaches $.190$. Direct GAR with successor auxiliaries reaches $.440$. Five-seed completion and checkpoint-level local metrics are needed before attributing the gap to geometry shaping.

The decisive analysis will measure pairwise ranking accuracy, top-1 recall, mean regret, Spearman correlation with exact continuation cost, and the optimal-versus-best-suboptimal margin for every retained checkpoint and seed. It will then correlate each quantity with strict success. The theoretical account predicts that local ranking and margin will track planning more closely than one-step latent error, successor retrieval, effective rank, or global probe scores.

\subsection{Global rank is not a planning explanation}

Table~\ref{tab:probes-main} reports the completed frozen-feature diagnostics. Both JEPA spaces are non-collapsed, with effective ranks near 200 in 256 dimensions. The token LM has effective rank $3.55$ and remains competitive in planning. Effective rank is therefore a health check, not a control metric.

Sentence representations make operation identity much more linearly accessible than the JEPA states. MLP JEPA strongly exposes resolved-variable count. That variable is confounded with elapsed step index, so it cannot yet be interpreted as semantic progress. Remaining distance is moderately decodable across all systems. Numerical outcomes are weakly decoded. The existing necessary-action probe is at chance and is not used as positive evidence.

\subsection{A progress-plus-residual hypothesis}

A representation may decompose as
\begin{equation}
    z=z_{\mathrm{progress}}+z_{\mathrm{residual}}.
\end{equation}
A small goal-relative component can govern local action ordering while many tangential directions retain predictive detail. This would reconcile high JEPA rank with a simple planning readout. We will test the hypothesis by predicting remaining distance from the scalar goal distance, the GAR energy, the full state, and the residual after removing the estimated goal-progress direction. The hypothesis is supported only if removing that direction selectively damages action ordering while leaving other predictive probes intact.

\subsection{Controlled semantic interventions}

An ordinary t-SNE or UMAP cloud cannot establish the quotient account. The required plot must use controlled pairs. We will encode identical semantic states under paraphrase, identical actions under paraphrase, high-overlap negations with changed consequences, operator swaps, irrelevant entity renaming, and histories matched on step index but not true progress. Full-space retrieval, pairwise distance, and action-order preservation are primary. A shared UMAP and t-SNE projection will illustrate the same frozen points and use identical hyperparameters across JEPA and LM representations.

These paired features have not yet been collected. We therefore omit a manifold plot rather than presenting an uncontrolled projection as evidence. The required outcome is clear: semantic paraphrases should retrieve one another, while a negation should separate only when it changes the controlled future. Failure on either side would contradict the quotient interpretation.
