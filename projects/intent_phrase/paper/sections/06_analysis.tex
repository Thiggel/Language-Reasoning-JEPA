\section{Representation Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Local action order.}
RQ3 concerns within-state action ordering rather than global cluster structure. In a one-seed aligned-history audit, the deployed transition-value energy attains $.826$ pairwise accuracy and $.786$ top-1 optimal-action recall. Raw predicted successor distance attains $.603$ and $.549$. The learned energy orders actions far better than uncalibrated latent distance in that checkpoint.

The audit does not yet isolate representation shaping from supervised readout. Full predictive GAR reaches $.435$ strict success in the matched seed. Detaching GAR gradients from the predictive body reaches $.375$, raw-distance selection reaches $.190$, and direct GAR with successor auxiliaries reaches $.440$. Five-seed completion and checkpoint-level local metrics are needed before assigning the full gap to changes in the predictive representation.

Because the audit covers one seed, it does not establish whether local ordering tracks planning across checkpoints or runs. It also does not establish that GAR reshapes the predictor rather than training a stronger readout.

\paragraph{Global decodability.}
Table~\ref{tab:probes-main} reports the completed frozen-feature diagnostics for RQ4. Both JEPA spaces are non-collapsed, with effective ranks near 200 in 256 dimensions. Token-LM features have effective rank $3.55$ while supporting competitive planning. Effective rank is therefore a health check rather than an explanation of control performance.

Sentence representations make operation identity substantially more linearly accessible than JEPA representations. MLP JEPA strongly exposes resolved-variable count, but resolved count is confounded with elapsed step index. Remaining distance is moderately decodable across all systems. Numerical outcomes are weakly decoded. The existing necessary-action probe is at chance and provides no positive evidence.
