\section{Conclusion}

Controlled-future equivalence specifies which language histories may share a planning state. Action-conditioned prediction models transitions between such states, but does not determine how their consequences should be ordered. GAR supplies that local order supervision.

On stylized iGSM, predictive GAR turns a weak latent-prediction policy into a viable conditional planner. Counterfactual ordering contributes the larger gain, and successor prediction adds a further improvement. The strongest sentence model remains better in strict success. Raw distance also remains much weaker than the learned GAR energy.

Direct GAR matches the predictive policy in the current one-seed comparison. The experiments therefore do not yet show that a policy must factor through its predicted successor. They support the narrower claim that successor prediction supplies useful auxiliary training under matched order supervision.

The present evidence concerns conditional planning under a supplied feasible-action menu on a synthetic arithmetic domain. It does not yet support claims about unrestricted language agents or general-purpose reasoning.
