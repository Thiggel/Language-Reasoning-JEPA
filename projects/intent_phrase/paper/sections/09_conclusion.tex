\section{Conclusion}

Action-conditioned joint-embedding prediction can support nontrivial language-action planning without reconstructing the next sentence. The result is competitive under a common feasible-action interface, but it does not surpass the strongest sentence baseline. The scientific contribution lies in the mechanism. Next-state prediction alone does not produce useful action selection. GAR supplies local counterfactual order constraints and a learned transition-value score, and both ranking and successor supervision matter.

The same controls sharpen the open question. Raw goal distance is weak, a direct ranker matches the current JEPA in one seed, and deeper latent rollout amplifies error. A persuasive account of JEPA reasoning must therefore show where predictive factorization helps, connect local ordering geometry to closed-loop behavior, and succeed under semantic and compositional interventions. The draft makes these obligations visible so that later results can strengthen or revise the claim without changing the evaluation contract.

The current claim is limited to conditional planning under a symbolic feasible-action menu on stylized iGSM. Cross-domain evidence and an unrestricted proposal interface remain future work.
