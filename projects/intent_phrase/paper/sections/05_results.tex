\section{Planning results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Latent prediction supports competitive planning}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} and Figure~\ref{fig:headline} report the completed five-seed comparison. MLP JEPA reaches $43.3\%$ strict success and has the smallest seed variability among learned models. Its mean is close to the token LM at $42.2\%$ and below the sentence LM at $47.1\%$. The sentence-plus-latent model leads strict success at $52.1\%$. The causal JEPA reaches $36.6\%$, so replacing the MLP predictor with the present causal Transformer does not improve the recipe.

These results support viability rather than superiority. They also suggest that generative and latent-predictive objectives can be complementary. The best strict result comes from a sentence model that combines them.

\subsection{Next-state prediction is not enough}

GAR is the clearest causal result. Removing GAR reduces strict success from $.433$ to $.076$. Training only factual and counterfactual latent MSE reaches $.183$. Ranking without direct advantage regression reaches $.358$. Removing counterfactual latent prediction while retaining GAR reaches $.345$. Thus ordering supervision and successor prediction provide complementary gains, with ordering responsible for the larger share.

The advantage horizon is not monotonic. Horizon two reaches $.433$, while horizons one and four reach $.161$ and $.181$. This result concerns the current reference-continuation target and does not establish a general optimal horizon. Increasing the number of counterfactual alternatives from one to four yields a modest change from $.403$ to $.439$. Direct advantage weights of $.10$ and $.50$ reach $.443$ and $.402$, which argues against a single fragile coefficient accounting for the full effect.

\subsection{Meaningful counterfactual labels matter}

A shuffled-GAR control preserves the counterfactual examples but randomizes their quality labels. Across three learning rates it reaches $.015$ strict success. A direct ranker without successor auxiliaries reaches $.150$ to $.185$ across the same local learning-rate screen. By comparison, the matched full GAR seed reaches $.435$, and the direct ranker with JEPA successor auxiliaries reaches $.440$. These controls rule out the simple explanation that GAR works only because it supplies more alternative transitions. They also show that successor auxiliaries improve a direct scorer even when the predicted successor is not its decision bottleneck.

\subsection{Current predictive factorization claim is limited}

One-seed controls localize the mechanism but do not settle it. Detached GAR gradients reach $.375$ strict success, compared with $.435$ for full GAR. Geometry-only planning reaches $.190$. The direct ranker reaches $.440$. The predictive transition-value score attains $.786$ top-1 optimal-action recall and $.826$ pairwise accuracy. Raw predicted geometry reaches $.549$ and $.603$.

The learned score is therefore much more useful than Euclidean goal distance. GAR gradients into the predictive body appear helpful in one seed, but a direct discriminative path remains equally strong on in-distribution iGSM. A unique benefit of predictive factorization must be demonstrated through data efficiency, compositional length, action paraphrases, goal transfer, or test-time simulation.

\subsection{Deeper simulation currently amplifies error}

Figure~\ref{fig:ttc} presents the repaired fixed-checkpoint gate. MLP JEPA falls from $.471$ at depth one to $.106$ at depth two and $.135$ at depth four. The direct ranker falls from $.432$ to $.122$ and $.085$. Zero and shuffled controls remain close to the random-policy rate, so the repaired evaluator removes the earlier terminal-path artifact. Additional computation does not improve the current models. The result instead identifies rollout drift or off-distribution score calibration as the next mechanism to solve.
