\section{Can latent prediction plan?}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Viability under a common action interface}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} answers RQ1. The MLP JEPA with GAR attains $.433$ strict success. Its mean is close to the token LM at $.422$, below the sentence LM at $.471$, and below the sentence-plus-latent model at $.521$. The causal JEPA attains $.366$. The MLP JEPA has the smallest seed standard deviation among the learned methods, although five seeds do not establish a general stability advantage.

The result establishes that a non-generative latent consequence model can support nontrivial closed-loop planning. It does not establish that JEPA is better than language modeling. The leading sentence-plus-latent row instead suggests that generative and predictive objectives can provide complementary biases.

\subsection{Prediction alone is not sufficient}

Table~\ref{tab:central-ablation} gives the central empirical result. Factual and counterfactual latent MSE without GAR attains $.183$ strict success. The full predictive GAR model attains $.433$. A factual-prediction-only configuration, labeled no GAR in the run ledger, attains $.076$. Ranking without pairwise regression attains $.358$. Removing the counterfactual latent loss while retaining GAR attains $.345$.

These rows support two conclusions. Same-state action ordering accounts for the larger part of the planning gain. Counterfactual successor prediction adds information beyond the ordering labels. The full gain is not explained by additional alternative transitions alone.

The target horizon is not monotonic. Horizons one, two, and four attain $.161$, $.433$, and $.181$ strict success. This is a property of the present geometric reference-continuation target, not a general claim that two-step supervision is optimal. Increasing counterfactual breadth from one to four changes strict success from $.403$ to $.439$. Pairwise-regression weights $.10$ and $.50$ attain $.443$ and $.402$. Nearby coefficients therefore preserve much of the effect.

\subsection{Information-matched controls}

Shuffling the geometric quality labels while retaining counterfactual transitions gives $.015$ strict success at each of three screened learning rates. A direct GAR scorer without successor auxiliaries reaches $.150$ to $.185$. The same direct GAR policy path with JEPA successor auxiliaries reaches $.440$ in one seed, compared with $.435$ for transition-value GAR in that seed. These controls show that the useful signal is not simply the number of counterfactual examples. They also prevent a stronger conclusion. In-distribution performance does not yet show that routing the policy through a predicted successor is better than direct GAR scoring.

The appropriate next test is not another leaderboard comparison. It is a matched comparison under data scarcity, longer compositions, action paraphrases, altered goals, and stable multi-step simulation. A benefit on one of these axes would support the predictive factorization rather than GAR supervision alone.
