\section{Language-mediated predictive control}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Language as a non-canonical interface}

Consider a controlled process
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},P,\Omega,O,c,\mathcal{G}).
\end{equation}
The semantic world state is $s_t\in\mathcal{S}$ and the semantic intervention is $u_t\in\mathcal{U}$. The agent observes language $x_t\in\Omega$ and chooses a language action $a_t$. A grounding map $\alpha$ associates the phrase with an intervention, $\alpha(a_t)=u_t$. Language is non-canonical: different wording may express the same state or intervention, irrelevant facts may be present, and task-relevant facts may only become clear through history.

The agent's information is therefore
\begin{equation}
    h_t=(x_0,a_0,x_1,\ldots,a_{t-1},x_t).
\end{equation}
Our experiments use a deterministic, finite-horizon specialization. The transition $T(h,a)$ appends the environment's deterministic consequence of $a$ to $h$. This restriction matters because a point predictor in a general stochastic process can represent a conditional mean rather than a complete multimodal future.

\subsection{Predictive state as a quotient of histories}

Predictive state representations define state through action-conditioned predictions of future observations rather than recovery of a privileged hidden parameterization \citep{littman2001predictive}. We adapt that view to language-mediated control. Let $Y$ contain future task-relevant observations and terminal outcomes, $C$ future costs, and $F$ future feasible-action sets. Two histories are \emph{controlled-predictively equivalent} when every admissible policy induces the same future law. The policies below range over grounded action continuations that are admissible from both histories:
\begin{align}
h \sim_{\mathrm{pred}} h'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(Y_{t+1:\tau},C_{t:\tau},F_{t:\tau}\mid h)
=
\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(Y_{t+1:\tau},C_{t:\tau},F_{t:\tau}\mid h')
\quad\forall\pi.
\label{eq:predictive-equivalence}
\end{align}
In the deterministic testbed, this says that every semantically admissible action continuation has the same task-relevant consequences. The ideal predictive state is the equivalence class
\begin{equation}
    q(h)=[h]_{\sim_{\mathrm{pred}}}\in\mathcal{H}/{\sim_{\mathrm{pred}}}.
\end{equation}
This quotient formalizes the desired invariances. Paraphrased histories with identical controlled futures should meet. Negation and operator changes should remain separate when they change a consequence. An irrelevant entity rename may be removed. A representation that merges histories with different controlled futures is insufficient.

\begin{proposition}[Predictive equivalence is control sufficient]
If $h\sim_{\mathrm{pred}}h'$ includes equality of future costs and terminal events under every admissible policy, then $V^\pi(h)=V^\pi(h')$ for every policy $\pi$. Hence $V^\star(h)=V^\star(h')$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The return is a measurable function of the future cost and terminal sequence. Equation~\ref{eq:predictive-equivalence} gives the same distribution of that sequence under each policy, and therefore the same expected return. Taking the optimum over policies preserves equality.
\end{proof}

The proposition identifies what a sufficient state must preserve. It is standard in spirit. Bisimulation gives a recursive local account through agreement in immediate reward and transitions between equivalence classes \citep{ferns2011bisimulation}. Our claim is weaker than learning a complete bisimulation metric. GAR constrains selected, goal-conditioned action orderings.

\subsection{Compression is a desideratum, not an implemented objective}

The quotient is the coarsest exact partition that preserves the controlled futures in Equation~\ref{eq:predictive-equivalence}. This supplies a principled compression narrative without asserting that our neural model finds the minimal quotient. The information bottleneck seeks a compressed code of an input that retains information about a target \citep{tishby2000information}. A controlled predictive analogue would trade $I(H;Z)$ against information about future outcomes conditional on future actions. Our method does not optimize such a rate term. Latent width and effective rank are not evidence of an explicit information bottleneck.
