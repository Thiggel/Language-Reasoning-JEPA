\begin{abstract}
Agents acting through language must compare consequences rather than merely generate plausible text. We study action-conditioned joint-embedding prediction in a controlled language planning environment. Controlled-future equivalence defines a state sufficient for decision making, yet accurate transition prediction leaves its metric underdetermined. Two latent coordinate systems can encode identical transitions and reverse the ordering of actions by goal distance. We introduce Geometric Advantage Ranking (GAR), which learns same-state order constraints from observed counterfactual consequences. GAR may supervise a scorer over predicted successors or an information-matched direct scorer. On stylized arithmetic dependency puzzles, predictive GAR solves $43.3\%$ of episodes in the minimum number of actions across five seeds, compared with $18.3\%$ for latent prediction alone. The strongest sentence model reaches $52.1\%$. Five-seed ablations attribute the larger gain to counterfactual ordering and a further gain to successor prediction. A local audit finds substantially better action ordering from the learned GAR energy than from raw latent distance, while global effective rank does not track planning. In this environment, predictive state is a viable substrate for reasoning only when training also identifies how action consequences should be ordered.
\end{abstract}
