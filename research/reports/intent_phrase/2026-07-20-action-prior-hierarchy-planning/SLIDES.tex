\begin{frame}{The concrete question}
\begin{itemize}
\item The model chooses a natural-language instruction, and the environment executes it.
\item A supervised policy prior proposes likely next instructions.
\item JEPA predicts latent consequences and reranks those proposals.
\item Success means solving within the minimum action count, or with two extra actions.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The prior is stronger than current JEPA reranking}

\vspace{1em}

\begin{itemize}
\item What to notice: every nonzero in-range cell favors prior-only planning.
\item Why it matters: current latent energy removes value rather than adding it.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Deep rollout helps only with privileged future menus}
\begin{itemize}
\item At length nine, strict top-four success rises from .061 at depth one to .439 at depth four.
\item With two extra actions, the same comparison rises from .750 to .994.
\item Future feasible actions came from the hidden dependency graph, so this is an oracle diagnostic.
\item Wide hierarchical CEM loses to the first-feasible rule at every tested bottleneck.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Next decision: separate two kinds of difficulty}
\begin{itemize}
\item The old curve changed reasoning length and prompt size at the same boundary.
\item We will vary prompt variables while holding reasoning length at nine.
\item We will vary reasoning length from nine to twelve while holding prompts at 24--36 variables.
\item Only then will we train a deployable predicted-state future-action proposal model.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
